The first of the month was my 29th birthday! It was another quiet celebration this year, in no small part because of the heatwave rolling through town; I'm not much inclined to go on a romp about the city when it's 117 F out. The day wasn't completely lost to the overwhelming heat though! My wish for this year was a trip to Bookmans, my favorite secondhand store - it's a bit of a hike from our house, on the worst highway in the city to boot, so I don't ask to go very often.

It was as busy in there as ever but the crowd didn't bother me too much this time, thankfully. They've got a little bit (well, a lot bit) of everything in there: books of all shapes and sizes and subjects, massive collections of vinyls and CDs, an entire area sectioned off just for used instruments and scorebooks, retro video games and hardware, old movie posters (and old movies! even stuff on VHS!), a 10-foot long wall stacked top to bottom with jigsaw puzzles, a case of anime figures packed to the brim with Mikus, cards from just about every TCG in existence, a whole section just for vintage glasses and mugs and kitchenware, and more tchotchkes scattered around than you can imagine. The store layout is dense and a bit nonsensical but that's my favorite part; I love wandering through the maze-like shelves and sniffing out all the hidden nooks and crannies, like finding little treasure spots. I think I could spend a whole day inside and not see everything squirreled away, waiting for the right hands to find them and bring them home...

I did have a small shopping list, though I had no expectations of anything being in stock given the nature of secondhand stores. Maybe it would be better called a hoping list?

  • a copy of Bird by Bird, ideally the 25th anniversary edition as the font in the previous printing is a bit too small for my eyes to read comfortably
  • any Japanese language books! They have a whole section for foreign language books, it's fun to peruse the shelves. I don't think they've ever had books specifically for learning Japanese that caught my eye (it's most often beginner stuff, which I'm far past) but they usually have a few secondhand novels and manga mixed in there
  • The Red Winter. Hardcover and secondhand, in my ideal world, but that would be a miracle given it only released a few months ago
  • some sort of small frog statuette for our bathroom (vitally important)

Unfortunately we found no frog friends suitable for our needs (though we did meet a very snazzy, jazzy gecko) so the hunt continues. Everything else: great success! My favorite find was the second volume of Trigun Maximum in excellent condition; I about lost my mind when I pulled it off the shelf. This book is almost as old as me.

They let me take him home for a mere seven dollars... they didn't even know what they had...

My partner Adrian and I spent a while in the poetry and drama section together; we've both expressed interest in reading more poetry recently. He misses it from his English major days and I've just never really tried before. I made big wet eyes at him to pick up a copy of Macbeth (the same edition I read in high school, a fun coincidence) before we split off to wander around again. I made my way over to the manga and graphic novels - their selection is overflowing but nothing caught my eye on this trip.

Afterwards, we made an impromptu stop at Daiso so I picked up several sorely-needed packs of index tabs and some of my favorite bread. Then it was over to our beloved local ice cream shop for a birthday treat (we got an extra waffle bowl of ice cream as a gift! yippie!) and back home.

I got birthday cake flavor because I think I'm funny and I have a killer sweet tooth.

For the last few years it's been tradition for me to order cookie cake from a local bakery, but they unfortunately closed a few months ago. I wasn't keen on baking my own cake this year, so I was at a loss for what my birthday cake should be... Adrian, in his infinite kindness, made me a big batch of Oreo truffles that morning as a surprise. I forgot to take a picture but they were very yummy, as always :]

I was humbled to receive a few gifts from friends and family this year. I never expect anything now that I'm very firmly out of kid birthday territory, but that just makes the surprise of receiving something all the sweeter. Adrian gifted me the artbook for Witch Hat Atelier; for months I've longed for a copy both to study and to admire, but never would commit to a purchase. To hold in my hands proof that he noticed and remembered made my heart soar (plus, it truly is a beautiful book).

(slapping the cover) they can fit so many Qifreys in this bad boy

My fishy friend surprised me with another something I've put off buying for an age: a mini-sized craft iron, to make pressing seams on doll-sized clothes a little less harrowing. As a silly treat, he also gave me my new favorite pen: a fish-shaped, blue-ink ballpoint whom I christened Anchovio. And finally, the most unexpected was a message from my coy friend, who sent along their well-wishes and a little drawing of the character I play in our tabletop game. My little Cel now greets me with their big glass of water and even bigger grin every time I unlock my phone :]

One of the fun things about getting older: you can buy yourself as many birthday gifts as you want. Who's gonna stop you? This year I got myself two things (not counting the spoils from our Bookmans outing). First: one of the new Tamagotchi Paradise shells! I chose White Glacier. The periwinkle blue makes me very happy :] I forgot to get a set of rechargeable AAAs though, so I haven't cracked it open yet. Second is a new tradition I'm starting for myself, and it requires a bit of context; allow me to spin you a yarn, for a moment.

On my birthday last year, I asked to stop by our local Book-Off so Adrian and I could take a couple turns on their gachapon machines. While we idly wandered through the aisles of used figures, a curious box caught my eye. Royal blue and shimmering gold with an classic, understated design. I couldn't tell you what the Mandarin on the back says but in English it declared this is Vol.003 of the TEA TIME CATS by Ribose, part of the Decorated Life Collection Series and released in 2023. It had no plastic window to peek into and only the silhouette of a teapot printed on the outside. Nothing else to suggest what was hidden within - other than some sort of cat, I had to assume, most likely related to tea and/or time. Sealed, no missing parts, only one corner of the box a bit scuffed. It whispered to something in my soul, in much the same way that real cats do when they choose you as their new owner; I would be going home with a TEA TIME CAT that day.

(It didn't occur to me until later that evening that I could have looked it up on MFC or somewhere similar LOL. It was hot as hell and I was nursing the start of a miserable head cold, cut past me a little slack, yeah?)

The drive home was excruciating, both because I was dying to peek inside the box and because my throat hurt something fierce (we stopped by eegee's on the way for shaved ice). I beelined for the boxcutter as soon as I had it settled on the kitchen table, cutting through the tape holding the flap closed with fumbling fingers. And then I fell in love.

The gasp I gosped when I pulled her out of the box...

A beautiful young girl, her silky black pigtails speckled with stars, leaning on the rim of the teapot that matches her sailor dress. She regards you with curiosity, ears forward and tail aloft. If you asked politely, she might even show you the soft toe beans on her paws. You can pose her outside of the pot, too, if you'd like, with her resting her little hands upon a melting ice cube as she tilts her head up at you. Her teapot is indeed real porcelain; if you so wished, you could brew yourself enough tea for a strong cup or two (I don't think much more water would fit).

I spent the rest of the day with her on my desk, unable to stop myself from glancing over every few minutes. So many of my favorite things wrapped up into one little figure; finding her on the shelf that day was nothing short of pure serendipity. And lo and behold—after it finally dawned on me to look her up online—she had sisters.

All the currently released tea cats, including two limited edition recolors! Ribose (the manufacturer) have teased some upcoming new sculpts but they don't have release dates yet.

That is, unfortunately, where the story paused for the better part of a year. I'm not much of a figure person to begin with, so much as I loved her (and as adorable as her sisters are) I couldn't justify going on a hunt to track them all down secondhand. I have some trouble letting myself collect things, as well... So she's sat on my shelf ever since, without anyone to keep her company. I don't regret bringing her home, but every so often I wondered if she felt lonely, sitting there by herself.

Jump ahead to about a week before my birthday this year, and an idea hit me: why not bring one of her sisters home every year on my birthday, until I've collected them all?

I doubted lightning would strike twice at that same local Book-Off but surely, between scouring MFC classifieds and Rakuten auctions and AmiAmi's preowned section, I could find people out there looking to give their tea time cats a new home. Only adding one a year to my collection wouldn't feel like such an overindulgence (and it would be much kinder on the wallet, besides). And so a new tradition was born!

A bit of idle searching every night before bed brought me to the eldest of her sisters three days after my birthday. The box she calls home is a rich pine green, and this time there's even a proper piece of art! A grey and white longhair cat with a charming collar and bow, framed by golden flowers as she watches a butterfly float closer. The seller was diligent, taking pictures of the box from every angle to confirm its authenticity. The only thing they didn't take a picture of the figure itself; the box came to them already opened but the tea cat inside was never put on display, so factory tape and plastic still held everything sealed. They thought it prudent not to break the seal just for a picture. The wait was, again, excruciating, but at least no head cold this time.

Her little songbird friend is so cute.

She rests in her box for now; I have a display case but it needs a good cleaning before I put anything new inside. Her little sister seems quite excited knowing she won't be alone on the shelf anymore.

Together at last! The difference in their scale is a bit silly but I don't mind it.

I've done no small amount of pondering leading up to my birthday, on aging and my dreams and whether I'm content with the person I am today... most of my thoughts are a little too personal for me to comfortably share on the web, but I will say this: I'm grateful that I get to wake up every day. Even on the bad days, and the spoonless days, and the days when I'd rather do anything but exist, I'm still glad that I won the impossible cosmic lottery and I get to wake up and experience life. I'm happy that I am alive. I never thought I would know how that feels, but now that I do I never want to let it go.

Hooray for another trip around the sun!
